4 colors or 5?

Should the master "Joyful Clinics" brand have its own signature color, distinct from the four service lines? Or does the parent brand stay neutral, letting the service lines carry the color identity?

Things to Consider

Case for 5 colors (Option A)

  • The main website, shared marketing, and business cards get a distinctive color that isn't confused with any service line
  • When a patient sees pink, they know it's the parent brand. When they see blue, they know it's telehealth. Clear wayfinding.
  • Scales better if new service lines are added later — the master color stays constant
  • Pink stands out against the blue/orange/purple/green palette — high visual contrast

Case for 4 colors (Option B)

  • Simpler system — fewer colors to manage across brand materials
  • The Juno Vet approach: one unified brand, differentiation happens through content/location, not color
  • The parent brand stays authoritative and understated — the service lines get to shine
  • Less risk of the palette feeling "too colorful" or carnival-like

Website implications

With 5 colors, the main joyfulclinics.com homepage has its own strong identity. Service line pages shift to their color. Navigation uses the master color as the anchor.

With 4 colors, the homepage is more of a "directory" — neutral frame, colorful service line cards. Each service line subsite has its own personality.

Signage & physical space

If clinics co-locate multiple services, a 5th master color helps tie the physical space together. The front door says "Joyful Clinics" in pink, and interior wayfinding uses service colors.

With 4 colors, the building exterior would need to pick one service color or go neutral.

*

Inspiration: Juno Veterinary

Multi-location vet brand with unified green identity. Locations (Toronto, Vancouver, Burlington) share one color system. Differentiation happens through location pages, not color.
