How should color work across the brand?

Joyful Clinics is the parent brand. Locations (like New Hampshire) each offer a specific service type. The question is whether color differentiates service types, and if so, does the master brand get its own color too?

A: One Color B: 4 Colors C: 5 Colors
A

One Color — Unified Brand

Every clinic, every location, every service type uses the same brand color. Differentiation happens through the service name and location, not color. Like Juno Vet's single-green approach.

Considerations

Option A: One Color

  • Simplest to manage — one color across everything
  • Strong, unified brand recognition
  • Service type communicated by name only, not visually
  • Harder to differentiate at a glance as locations grow

Option B: 4 Colors

  • Service types are instantly recognizable by color
  • Master brand stays understated — service lines shine
  • The homepage is a neutral "directory" framing
  • Simpler than 5 colors, but still has visual wayfinding

Option C: 5 Colors

  • Maximum differentiation — every brand level has its own color
  • Master brand has a strong, ownable identity (pink)
  • Most complex to manage across materials
  • Pink homepage → orange location page is a clear visual shift
*

Inspiration: Juno Veterinary

Multi-location vet brand using a unified single-color approach. Locations (Toronto, Vancouver, Burlington) share one color. Differentiation through location pages, not color.
